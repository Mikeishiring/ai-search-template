Making the next owner clear

Library requests arrived by email, at the desk and in a shared notebook. I mapped the handoffs and introduced a weekly review with the coordinators. They continued using it after the trial; we did not measure turnaround.

  • No baseline or turnaround measurement was collected.
  • This account covers one library team and is self-reported.

    Practice

    Traced request routes and revised the shared list after coordinator feedback.

    Assistance Coordinators defined the handoff rules; AI involvement is not recorded.

Context & evidence

Context

Requests crossed between the front desk, events and facilities. People could see that something needed doing, but the next owner was often implicit.

Aim

Make ownership visible without asking volunteers to learn another application.

My contribution

I traced the existing routes, drafted a shared request list and facilitated the first reviews. I revised the list after coordinators found its status labels unclear.

Decisions

  • Used the team's existing spreadsheet so the trial did not depend on a new tool.
  • Separated the next owner from the person who first received the request.
  • Removed a priority field after the team interpreted its labels differently.

Shared work

Contributor 02 proposed the weekly review. The coordinators defined the handoff rules and kept the list current. I designed and facilitated the trial.

Outcome

The coordinators kept the weekly review after the trial. That is evidence of continued use, not a measured improvement in speed.

02
May–July 2030

A guide tested at the front desk

I turned recurring volunteer questions into a short desk guide. Contributor 03 checked the procedures, and two volunteers tried it during supervised shifts. Their feedback improved the guide; independent use was not tested.

  • Two volunteers tried the guide with supervision.
  • No time saving or change in error rate was measured.
Context & evidence

Context

Experienced volunteers answered the same practical questions during busy shifts. Existing notes were distributed across several documents.

Aim

Put the routine steps in one place while keeping escalation routes visible.

My contribution

I grouped the questions, drafted the guide and observed the trial. I rewrote the room-booking instructions when volunteers could not tell which exceptions needed a coordinator.

Decisions

  • Organized the guide around tasks, rather than the team's internal structure.
  • Kept policy exceptions beside the relevant steps.
  • Used a printable format because the shared desk computer was not always available.

Shared work

Contributor 03 verified the procedures. The volunteers identified unclear instructions; the coordinator retained responsibility for training and policy.

Outcome

The revised guide was retained for the next induction. The trial does not establish that volunteers could work independently or that support time fell.

Context & evidence

Context

The library had no shared way to see which event equipment was out on loan. A digital log was one proposed response.

Aim

Test whether a simple checkout history would answer the team's everyday questions.

My contribution

I sketched the flow, asked AI to generate a Python prototype and checked checkout and return with fictional items. I cannot write Python independently. I have not connected it to real records or arranged a user trial.

Decisions

  • Used fictional test items during prototyping.
  • Kept the first version limited to checkout and return.
  • Left reminders out until the team decides who would own follow-up.

Shared work

Contributor 02 described the current process. The coordinator will decide whether a trial is useful; no team adoption is claimed.

Outcome

A local prototype exists. User testing, maintenance ownership and deployment remain open.

Still to clarify

  • Would a shared paper log solve the problem more simply?
