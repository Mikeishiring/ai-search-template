# Work, skills and search

## Capture work first

Use the work accounts as the durable record, including small projects, experiments and work outside formal employment. Retain the problem, personal choices, implementation or coordination, collaborators, outcome and evidence. Gather relevant authorized material across sources, reconcile duplicate accounts, and connect repeated patterns without erasing differences or contradictions. Preserve existing IDs and the person's corrections.

Derive optional `capabilities` entries within an account only when its sources support them; see [format](format.md). Use `practice` for an activity such as process mapping and `tool` for something used to do it. Each entry records its application, assistance and limits in that specific work. Its evidence and date come from the parent account. Theme tags can aid retrieval but never establish proficiency. Leave the index absent when it adds nothing.

## Separate contribution from tool fluency

Record what the person did and what AI or other people supplied. A repository language, installed dependency, generated file or tool mention does not demonstrate the person's skill. Do not infer independent proficiency from assisted use, or lack of proficiency merely because AI was involved. Preserve explicit self-assessment as self-report. If support is unclear, record that uncertainty; ask only when resolving it would change the account or a role comparison.

For a person who says they use Python through AI but do not know Python, retain Python as a tool with AI assistance and the limit “Independent Python programming is not claimed.” Record activities such as problem framing, checking results or directing revisions only when their account or other evidence supports them. Those activities may matter more than the tool. Do not upgrade “ran a generated script” to “built automation systems,” or invent a measurable benefit.

Keep material assistance and limits beside claims in the work prose as well as the index, so a short resume or an export cannot detach the achievement from its actual basis. Never add proficiency bars, inferred years of experience or a flat list that presents assisted tools as independently held skills.

## Bridge to a later search

Keep the complete context; make a task-specific selection for each search. Use stated goals and constraints, demonstrated work, problems solved and supported practices to form search directions. Tool keywords are secondary. A query is a hypothesis about useful roles, not a new career fact.

For a supplied vacancy or promising role, follow [role comparison](role-comparison.md): connect the actual activities to account and source IDs, including timeline evidence outside the selected work index. Keep direct and transferable experience distinct from what the searched context does not establish. AI-assisted Python use can support exploring work involving AI-enabled prototyping when the accounts support that activity; it does not satisfy a requirement for independent Python development by itself.

Carry those distinctions into a search handoff, even when a connector only accepts skill keywords. Use a separate caveated brief or omit a misleading keyword rather than silently flattening it. Read actual role requirements before claiming fit. Carry newly supported work and corrections back into the maintained career document using the role-comparison workflow; keep vacancy-specific judgments and unestablished requirements separate. Context maintenance alone does not initiate a job search or write a candidate profile.
